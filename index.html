About
Precision science
meets machine learning

I am a tenured Associate Professor at Michigan State University's College of Human Medicine, jointly appointed in the Office of Medical Education Research and Development and the Department of Epidemiology and Biostatistics. My research identity centers on being a dual-trained psychometrician and biostatistician applying precision medicine methodology to precision medical education.

My methodological work bridges causal inference, individual treatment effects (ITE), latent variable modeling, and deep learning. I design ML-based evaluation systems — integrating NLP, computer vision, and LLM-based annotation — to analyze large-scale, multimodal behavioral data from clinical training environments.

As a Visiting Scholar at Stanford Medicine (2025), I collaborate with Ann Hsing's lab and interdisciplinary teams on scale development work for WELL of Life, interpretable ML frameworks for wearable data phenotyping, and longitudinal health modeling.

Prior to my academic career, I served in Taiwan's Ministry of Education (Bureau of International Cultural and Educational Relations) and hold degrees from Michigan State University, National Chengchi University (Taipei), and National Kaohsiung Normal University.

LLM-Based Annotation: GPT-4o Mini, RoBERTa & Human Raters

Designed and validated evaluation frameworks benchmarking LLM-based annotation against human raters using precision/recall, agreement metrics, and reliability models. Reduced labeling cost by 80% while maintaining performance through scalable human-in-the-loop pipelines.

AMA & NBME Funded · 2022–2025

Computer Vision for Clinical Behavioral Assessment in OSCEs

Built and validated action detection pipelines to automatically identify behavioral signals (hand-washing, glove use) across 150K+ medical student OSCE videos. Applied measurement invariance and fairness analyses across demographic subgroups. Presented at AAMC 2024 and AERA 2025.

Computer Vision Measurement Invariance Fairness OSCE

NBME Funded · 2023–2024

NLP Speech-to-Text for OSCE Clinical Competency Assessment

Developed domain-adapted speech-to-text models for transcribing 500K+ medical student–patient interactions in OSCEs. Built an NLP corpus and keyword analysis pipeline to extract clinical communication features, enabling automated, scalable scoring of history-gathering competencies.

NIH Funded · 2022–2024

Latent Class Analysis of Pelvic Pain Subgroups

Applied latent class analysis to identify clinically distinct pelvic pain subgroups differentially associated with endometriosis and inflammatory comorbidities in a multi-site dataset. Integrated self-reported symptom data, diagnostic codes, and comorbidity profiles to inform patient stratification and care-path prioritization. Published in Pain (IF 7.4, 2024).

LCA Patient Stratification NIH

Co-developed an interpretable framework for cohort-level phenotyping of longitudinal wearable sensor data, integrating statistical modeling with time-series features to characterize health and behavioral patterns. Manuscript submitted to IEEE; methods applicable to large-scale population health monitoring and personalized medicine pipelines.
